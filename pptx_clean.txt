Abstract
Heart disease remains a leading cause of mortality worldwide, emphasizing the need for accurate and early risk prediction to support preventive healthcare. While traditional machine learning techniques are widely used for clinical decision-making, their ability to model complex, high-dimensional medical data is often limited. This mini project presents a comparative study of classical and quantum-assisted machine learning models for early heart disease risk prediction. Three classical classifiers—Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and Support Vector Machine (SVM)—are evaluated alongside two quantum-assisted models: the Quantum Support Vector Machine (QSVM). Clinical and demographic data are pre-processed using normalization, feature scaling, and missing-value handling techniques. Quantum models are implemented in simulated quantum environments, where classical features are encoded into quantum states using quantum feature maps and optimized through hybrid classical–quantum learning. Model performance is compared using accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, and ROC-AUC. Results indicate that Random Forest performs strongly on full feature sets, while quantum-assisted models achieve competitive performance on reduced feature spaces by capturing non-linear feature relationships. In particular, VQC demonstrates stable  behavior with limited features, whereas QSVM is sensitive to feature encoding strategies. Despite current hardware constraints, this study highlights the potential of quantum-assisted learning as a complementary approach for future intelligent healthcare prediction systems.

Keywords: Heart Disease Prediction; Quantum-Assisted Machine Learning; Logistic Regression; Random Forest; Support Vector Machine; QSVM; VQC; Healthcare Analytics
Introduction
Heart disease is one of the leading causes of death worldwide, making early detection and prevention crucial. Machine learning (ML) techniques such as Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and Support Vector Machines have been widely applied to predict heart disease risk. However, these classical models often face challenges in handling complex medical data and capturing non-linear feature interactions.This mini project introduces a quantum-assisted machine learning framework for heart disease prediction. In addition to classical ML classifiers, quantum-inspired models like the Quantum Support Vector Machine (QSVM) are implemented using a publicly available heart disease dataset. Data preprocessing steps—normalization, scaling, and handling missing values—are applied to ensure reliable performance.Classical features are encoded into quantum states through quantum feature maps, and optimization is carried out using classical optimizers on quantum simulators. Model performance is assessed using accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, and ROC-AUC.The results show that quantum-assisted classifiers can effectively capture complex feature relationships and achieve competitive performance, even with fewer features. While current quantum hardware imposes limitations, this project highlights the potential of quantum-assisted approaches to enhance future healthcare prediction systems.

Hybrid Data Processing: Patient features are dynamically scaled and reduced using Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to accommodate quantum register limitations.

Classical Intelligence: Standard ensemble models (Random Forest, SVM, Logistic Regression) are trained in real-time to provide immediate benchmarks.
